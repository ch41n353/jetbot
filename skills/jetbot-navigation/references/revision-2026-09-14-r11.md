# Revision 11 — local object missions

Date:2026-09-14, America/Los_Angeles. Experimental and opt-in. The user requested
mission ownership on the robot rather than short movements followed by20-second
remote-planner waits. Earlier controllers remain available.

## Interface and behavior

`object_mission.py` receives one remotely identified target bounding box, a fresh
image/control generation, and an explicitly inspected static obstacle map.
It owns tracking, waypoint selection, motion and bounded recovery until arrival
or an exception. It does not recognize arbitrary object names or discover new
obstacles. A target label is supplied by the remote planner, not inferred locally.

In `maneuver_session.py`, use `plan_mission` with:

```json
{"command":"plan_mission","image_path":"EXACT_FRESH_CAPTURE_PATH","target_label":"Advil","target_box":[224,163,258,225],"standoff_cm":20,"inspected_free_rectangle_cm":[-40,-30,40,70],"obstacle_rectangles_cm":[]}
```

Those coordinates are an interface example, not the current scene or a reusable
clearance assertion. A complete route preview is created before execution.
Execute the returned plan with `{"command":"execute","plan":"RETURNED_PATH","mission":true}`.
Mission mode cannot combine with `spatial` or `batch`. CLI default validates
without motor access; powered CLI execution requires `--execute --log PATH`.

`observe` returns a fresh image while a worker runs, without replacing the
stationary planning anchor. `mission_status` reads the worker's atomic status
file. Neither cancels control. `stop` and `shutdown` retain their cancellation
behavior. Remote inspection latency does not block an already valid mission;
no remote API call exists inside the control loop. The result preserves the last
observed image and its timestamp; it is marked settled only after verified arrival.

## Continuous control and recovery

The mission retains one camera/IMU pose estimate. Forward motion proceeds through
internal waypoints without the old15/30cm or2/4second programmed stops. Camera
updates still gate every expiring motor command. Near the target, the measured
object bearing guides the final approach rather than a coarse search lattice.
Standoff accepts18–30cm and arrival requires a stable stopped window within3cm
of the requested estimated range.

Planning uses the existing inspected map and measured chassis/braking geometry.
Short lookahead samples speed/yaw uncertainty before forward commands. The new
optional `goal_heading_turns` search primitive improves alignment with a target.
Local searches occur with motors stopped, up to six searches per mission.
Reverse recovery remains unsupported; such a route requests remote review.
Unknown obstacles are not treated as newly detected free space.

Turning also differs from revision10: continuous generation-tagged motor holds
at0.14, bounded to roughly30degree bursts with angular-rate braking. Translation
and wrong-direction checks stop a burst beyond35degrees, below−3degrees in the
commanded direction, or after two seconds. Translation
stops before a turn; the next drive waits for angular settling. Forward duty is
0.16 with steering correction capped0.025. No background heartbeat sustains
motors through a vision failure. This new turning policy is not hardware validated.

`target_tracker.py` uses forward/backward optical flow and geometric inlier checks.
The original appearance template is immutable. A recent template can aid recovery
only after matching the original. Reacquisition requires a strong, unambiguous
match and consistency with the target's retained world position. The initial
measured target projection must also agree with the preview map.

Target loss sends zero motor output immediately. The controller keeps measuring
pose and searches locally for at most two seconds, requiring three good readings
and low estimated translation speed before resuming. At most two recovery entries
are allowed. Unresolved loss emits `remote_planner_required`; it does not silently
switch targets. `mission_complete` and local recovery events are recorded in
`LOG.events.jsonl`; `LOG.status.json` supports nonblocking inspection.

The global ceilings are180seconds and120cm travel. Existing180ms powered camera/renewal
deadlines,200ms motor leases, battery interlock, five-degree tilt, stall detection,
position sigma2cm and yaw sigma4degrees remain. Uncertainty is never reset just
to keep moving and can stop a mission well before those ceilings. This is not
proof of minutes of autonomy or unrestricted continuous operation.

Floor tracking now excludes the tracked object from its feature mask and uses
IMU yaw as well as tilt to initialize optical flow. The latter fixes insufficient
matches during faster turns; geometric and camera/IMU consistency checks remain.

## Evidence and remaining limits

`simulate_object_mission.py` renders an upright textured target and carpet and
runs the production mission/tracker against a2ms independent motion plant. It
adds35ms simulated target-processing cost alongside45ms floor processing, based
on the recorded-image benchmark. IMU attitudes are supplied directly; this does
not validate physical fusion, contact dynamics, or unrecognized obstacles.

`evaluate_object_missions.py` produced14 cases in
`reports/iteration-2026-09-14/object-mission-matrix.json`. Four of six ordinary
approaches completed; two angled approaches stopped on target-confidence loss.
Temporary occlusion was recovered locally and the mission completed. Persistent
loss, camera failure, slow camera, stall, cancellation during recovery and excess
processing all stopped. A retained-obstacle detour exhausted the local search
budget before movement. Every case ended with zero motor output and no audited
full-chassis-plus5cm clearance violation. Preserve failures alongside successes.

An aligned mission crossed internal waypoints in a powered interval longer than
six seconds with no scheduled waypoint stop. This demonstrates removal of the
four-second action boundary, not long-duration reliability.

Onboard replay of37 subsequent real Advil frames tracked all37 without loss:
median32.38ms, maximum61.05ms target-tracking time. This measured target processing
alone, not the complete control-loop deadline. See `mission-target-live-replay.json`.

The full test suite passed121 tests, including uninterrupted multi-waypoint
travel, local reacquisition, cancellation during recovery, ambiguous target
rejection, and non-interrupting supervisor observations.

No powered run of the new mission controller has been performed. A parallel
trial of the existing coalesced controller first rejected a stale frame while
tests consumed CPU. After pausing tests, it moved6.3cm and stopped at a5.54degree
IMU tilt change. The Advil was not reached. The tilt ramp was spread over several
frames; it cannot be labelled a harmless one-sample glitch. See
`live-tilt-replay.json`. Do not weaken the tilt guard without validating the
camera geometry and distinguishing chassis motion from IMU-mount motion.

Do not run CPU-heavy simulations or test suites alongside powered navigation.
Code editing can proceed concurrently; reserve compute for camera/IMU/control
during live trials. The user has explicitly prioritized live motion over tests
when the two compete for compute.

## Parallel review checkpoint

Three separate agents worked on target tracking, controller safety, and recorded
tilt diagnosis. Only the hardware task could access the robot; this checkpoint
used recorded data and no new motor commands.

Tracking now corrects accumulated crop drift with bounded registration against
the immutable original template. An independently recropped appearance score
must pass before accepting correction. Three targeted regression tests cover
drift, wrong identity, and duplicate ambiguity. The formerly failing angled
seed3 completed; seed1 recovered identity but exposed a controller stopping
transition. These follow-up results do not replace the earlier14-case matrix.
Measured tracker p95 is43–45ms; use at least45ms perception cost for subsequent
simulation. See `target-tracking-agent.json` for intermediate failures as well
as successes.

The final integrated seed1 follow-up, using45ms simulated perception cost,
completed in7.475seconds with one local recovery and no intermediate VLM call.
Estimated standoff was22.22cm and independent simulated range22.29cm, within the
existing3cm tolerance. It ended with zero motor output and no audited clearance
violation. The focused23-test integration suite passed before the final short
planning-stop timeout addition; the subsequent full-suite result is recorded
with the checkpoint report.

Search now requires0.2seconds of quiet estimated motion and fails if that stop
cannot be verified within one second. Only its next stopped observation can
span up to600ms; full VIO checks still apply and movement beyond0.1cm or0.5degree
rejects that refresh. Powered deadlines remain180ms. Clearance rejection waits
for coast to settle before retrying, with a one-second stop deadline and at most
three consecutive rejected launches. A stopped approach already within the
existing3cm arrival tolerance uses the same stable-arrival verification.
Recovery requires consecutive good readings within its two-second deadline.
Final stop failure overrides success and appends an explicit remote-planner event.

Recorded tilt diagnosis confirms the relative gravity comparison is invariant
to a fixed mounting rotation. Cross-gravity gyro rates reached23.4deg/s and
quiet post-stop gravity returned within0.99degree of the starting direction.
This supports transient assembly rotation but cannot separate chassis rocking
from mount movement. See `live-tilt-diagnosis.json`; the guard remains unchanged.
