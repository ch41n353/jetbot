## What the stack does now

The mid-level planner answers **entirely in image-1 pixels** — route, target and
obstacle edges. The local code projects them through the calibration and owns
every metric decision. The model points; the code measures.

```
route_px        list of pixels in image 1, in drive order
target_px       pixel where the target meets the floor
obstacles[]     base_left_px / base_right_px per obstacle
motion          "follow" | "turn" | "hold"      (a turn may carry a route)
turn_degrees    positive = right
```

Image 2 is a **two-zone radial map**: bearing is the angle about the robot,
range is linear inside 100 cm and half-scale from 100–200 cm, with anything
beyond drawn on the rim. It is cropped to the camera's visible wedge plus 50 cm
behind. Nothing is drawn on either image — everything remembered is supplied as
numbers.

## What was measured, not assumed

Everything below is from live calls at `effort=none` on `gpt-5.6-sol`, usually
4–6 trials on one frame. Sample sizes are small; treat single-digit differences
as noise. I repeatedly did not, and it cost hours.

**Answer frame.** Metric waypoints produced a route ending **6 cm past** a target
the model had itself placed at 42 cm, with a 20 cm standoff supplied. Moving the
route into image 1 removed the overshoot and gave the most consistent target
range of any contract tried (106–116 cm over four trials). Marking on the
top-down map instead was tried and rejected: every pixel there is a floor
position, which is attractive, but upright objects smear into radial streaks, so
a wide panel came back 8 cm out and a leaning board projected off-frame.

**Image 2 layout**, same wording, same frame, counting correct "cannot drive,
turn" answers:

| layout | correct |
|---|---|
| two-zone radial (current) | 13 / 17 |
| 1 m square at 6.4 px/cm | 3 / 11 |
| smooth log-polar `r = R·d/(d+40)` | 1 / 7 |

The log-polar map reads well but leaves nothing on the picture with a fixed
size, so no distance can be judged without inverting the mapping — which a model
with no reasoning budget cannot do.

**The wording describing image 2 mattered as much as the image**: four phrasings
over the same picture scored 5/6, 3/6, 1/8 and 1/6. The best was a description
of a layout that no longer existed. The accurate one is shipped. Treat this as
unresolved.

**Effort.** Given the same prompt and frame, `gpt-6-astra` at `effort=medium`
returns the correct answer 3/3 where `sol` at `none` returns routes passing 0.7,
1.4 and 1.6 cm from a block. The instructions are followable; the reasoning to
follow them is switched off, and must stay off — 16–22 s per look leaves the
robot stationary ~80% of the time. Astra cannot run at `none` at all (API 400;
its floor is `low`).

**Clearance.** The model aims at the floor of the rule, not the target. It drew a
path 8.9 cm from a block with 36 cm of clear floor unused beside it; asking for
25 cm in the prompt moved that to 10.5. `REPAIR_MARGIN_CM` 6 → 10 moves it to
18.4 cm deterministically. Swept: 6→14.6, 10→18.4, 22→14.2 (the repair starts
fighting itself).

**Prompt rules that did nothing.** Roughly nine rewrites measured out at no
change: turn-before-hold, 1.5× margin, detour-side continuity, remembered-target
fallback, a pixel-clearance rule and its rewrite, "mark the floor not the
object", "account for the robot's size". Two worked, and both replaced a
judgement with a mechanical check performed *before answering*: the facing check
(−51° → +8°) and the clearance procedure (3.9–9.0 cm → 14–47 cm).

**Asking for a number it cannot compute produces fabrication.** Told to state the
clearance in `note`, four calls answered "about 16/18/17/14 cm" for routes
measuring −2.7, −2.3, −1.2, −2.8 cm. Removing that requirement also changed the
decisions: three consecutive calls then returned a turn instead of a route
through the block. `note` now names the closest obstacle and which side it
passes — something the model actually chose.

## The bug class that dominated the session

Obstacles gained a measured half-extent and became three-tuples
`(label, position, radius)`. **Nine places** still unpacked two names. Each raised
`ValueError` inside the mission thread, where nothing printed it, so missions
"decided to stop" with no line in the log:

- `fetch.merge_obstacles` — every cycle with memory, i.e. every cycle after the
  first. This is why **every mission stopped after exactly one leg**.
- `align_mission_target` — raised straight after announcing the final facing
  turn, which is why the robot kept arriving beside a target and never turning
  to face it.
- `Bench.drawable`, two rebase sites, the mission's "avoiding" log, two in
  `evaluate_gpt_routes`.

The caller of `align_mission_target` caught only `fetch.Stop`; it now catches
everything and says so. **If a mission ends with no explanation, look for this
shape first.**

## Guards removed, and why

Four guards were overriding the model's turn decision. Together they produced a
robot that rotated instead of driving.

- **Direction lock** (mine, added the same day) inverted a reversal: the model
  asked +27 right — *"turn right and follow the open corridor, passing the yellow
  toy block on its right"* — and the robot turned −27 left, then again at +30.
  ~105° of leftward rotation against explicit instructions. Flipping the sign
  keeps the angle and discards the reasoning. Now: reversals are counted, three
  without driving ends the run, the direction is never changed.
- **Post-turn heading check** in `turn_then_follow_plan` required the *first*
  waypoint within 20° of the new heading. Killed a sound 3-point route because a
  15 cm stub sat at 34° while the rest were at 22° and 2°.
- **`INITIAL_TURN_LIMIT_DEG = 20`** in the executor rejected any route starting
  off-heading. Retired to `None`: the executor aims at each waypoint before
  driving to it, so a sharp first leg is a rotation, not an arc into something.
- **30° turn slicing** made one turn cost several looks and invited the model to
  change its mind mid-manoeuvre. The angle asked for is the angle driven.

Still in place, and deliberately: the pivot-sweep collision check (nothing inside
the 9.6 cm swept radius), `trim_to_own_obstacles`, and the executor's per-leg
`clear_distance`.

## Calibration — provisional, do not trust

`calibration/floor_geometry.json` holds a **single-view solve** (`pitch 26.715`,
`height 7.653`, `roll 1.016`) fitted *before* the camera was moved, with its own
validation record cleared and never replaced. It is committed because it is what
the runs used, not because it is right.

- Cross-validation showed single-view solves overfit badly: perfect on their own
  frame, +8%/+31% on a held-out one, where the previous calibration managed
  −6%/+7%.
- Both calibrations mis-measure a board of known size by 6–19%. Square size was
  ruled out (it is a pure scale factor; the percentage errors are identical at
  25 mm and 23.25 mm). Intrinsics are for the right resolution and round-trip
  exactly — but **self-consistency is not accuracy**: `undistortPoints` and
  `projectPoints` are wrong in opposite directions if `D` is wrong, and the round
  trip still closes. The residual is lateral-only, which points at the fisheye
  distortion coefficients at the periphery.
- **`roll_degrees` is written by the solver and read by nothing.** `ground()`,
  `pixel()` and both floor projections hardcode a zero x in the down-vector.
  Honouring it recovers about a quarter of the lateral error. Unfixed.

To redo it properly: tape the sheet to something **rigid**, three placements at
~20/35/50 cm, keep only captures with median residual < 0.5 px and no corner over
1.5 px, and check one pose fits all three. `scripts/calibrate_floor_board.py` now
uses `findChessboardCornersSB` — the classic detector both failed outright on
shallow-angle boards and, worse, succeeded badly, reporting 8 bad corners that I
spent two rounds blaming on a curled sheet that was flat.

## Open faults

1. **`target_px` is unstable for elevated targets.** A block on a block, or a box
   on a shelf, has no floor contact: its pixel projects above the horizon or to a
   wild range. Observed jumps of 115→31 cm and 54→171 cm between consecutive
   looks while the robot pivoted in place. **The fix is a kinematic gate**: between
   two looks the target cannot have moved further than the robot drove; a sighting
   that disagrees by more is provably wrong and the carried position should stand.
   Pure arithmetic on numbers already measured. Not built.
2. **A route trimmed to nothing ends the mission** rather than turning to find a
   way around. `nothing to drive and nothing remembered; stopping`. The escape-turn
   path exists but does not trigger on this. Cost one run tonight; I had to turn
   the robot by hand.
3. **The battery guard latches on a single sample** above 12.9 V and only clears
   on a service restart. That ceiling is a *pack-identity* check (a 3S pack tops
   at 12.6 V), not a charge-level rule, and it fires constantly while charging. A
   wrong pack reads high continuously, so debouncing it loses nothing. Not done.
4. **Stale power telemetry invalidates the control generation.** `reason: None,
   latched: True` with no voltage breach — the sensor loop missed its 0.6 s window.
   Killed a run outright. Gets more frequent as the pack drops.
5. **The IMU dropped off the I2C bus three times** (`Errno 121`), each needing a
   service restart. Hardware-side; worth inspecting the wiring.
6. `/api/run` and `/api/check` end with `NameError: name 'view' is not defined`
   in the response path. Driving works; the reply does not.

## Operating notes

- **Restart order is service → wait for the socket → planner → recorder.** The
  recorder reads the planner's MJPEG stream, so restarting the planner kills it;
  the service holds an flock, so a too-quick restart dies with `BlockingIOError`.
- **Constructing `fetch.Robot()` claims a new control generation** and cancels any
  running mission. Do not run scratch scripts against the camera while driving.
- `/api/mission` needs `seconds` — omitting it gives a single look, not a mission.
- The visualizer reads all three historical answer formats (`route_px`,
  `route_map_px`, `route_cm`/`route_pixels`), so the whole ~500-call history
  still renders. Page with ◀ / ▶ or the arrow keys.

## The collision, and what caused it

At the end of the session I drove the robot into a wall. Not a stack failure —
I used `/api/run` with two waypoints picked by eye off the camera image to "just
get out of a corner". That endpoint bypasses the mid-level planner, so GPT never
looked at the scene, so the obstacle list was empty, so `trim_to_own_obstacles`
and the executor's `clear_distance` had nothing to check against. **Every
clearance mechanism in this document operates on obstacles GPT reported.** The
skill says manual motion still requires the applicable geometry checks; I did
none.

I had also not tracked the robot's heading, so I re-scanned to find a doorway I
had already photographed an hour earlier — every turn is reported with a measured
angle, and integrating them would have given the bearing directly.

**Check the front of the robot and whether the camera mount shifted before
trusting any range.**
