# JetBot method iteration — morning report, 2026-09-14

**No robot movement or live hardware tests were performed after your instruction.**
The work used recorded frames and simulation. The main result is better evidence
for predictive braking, an optional faster tracking setting, and a fix for
confirming a stop at slow camera rates. This is not validation of unattended
physical navigation.

## What changed

- Built a reusable simulator that runs the actual route executor, carpet tracker
  and planar estimator against rendered camera frames and a simulated motor
  plant. Its position ground truth is independent of the controller's estimate.
- Added reproducible fault and calibration stress scenarios, recorded-frame
  benchmarks, and a separate braking-candidate evaluation.
- Added `--feature-budget 125` to the route tool. The default remains 250; 80 is
  also available experimentally. Motor power and clearance limits are unchanged.
- Reduced image-normalization intermediate buffers to float32. Tracker outputs
  were identical on the 41-pair comparison; the small timing difference is not
  strong enough to claim a reliable speedup from this change alone.
- Fixed the settling history: it now retains 400 ms, allowing three frames at
  170–180 ms spacing to confirm a stop. The old 300 ms history could never fit
  those frames. Stability thresholds and the one-second deadline are unchanged.
  An expired confirmation window now gives an explicit reason.

The completed work includes **212 simulation runs** across the paired and stress
campaigns below, plus **62 passing tests**. The separate smoke run is not included
in that count.

## Stopping accuracy: 60 paired simulated 5 cm routes

Each method ran on the same 60 synthetic plants. Speed varied from 8–16 cm/s,
coasting time constant from 35–120 ms, frame rate from 10–15 Hz, acquisition delay
from 5–35 ms, and pixel-noise sigma from 0–2. Simulated compute delay was 45 ms.
These ranges are hypotheses, not measurements of your robot's distribution.

| Method | Median absolute true error | 95th-percentile absolute error | Within 1 cm |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Stop after crossing target | 2.09 cm | 3.25 cm | 2/60 |
| Existing predictive braking | 0.69 cm | 1.62 cm | 45/60 |

Predictive braking improved the median but still overshot in 15 cases. Its worst
true error was 1.99 cm. All 15 were reported as overshoots, rather than arrivals,
in this correctly calibrated simulation. Neither method restarted motors after
its stop command. The legacy method reports a threshold crossing, which should
not be interpreted as confirmed arrival.

This paired campaign is frozen at source commit `53d12ee`, before the settling
history fix. That fix changes stop confirmation, not the braking commands.

and [source/parameter manifest](/home/jetbot/jetbot/local_nav/goals/route-simulation-2026-09-14.manifest.json).

## A more conservative braking candidate: 16 held-out pairs

A separate random seed tested an 80 ms coasting hypothesis, a 220 ms prediction
horizon cap and a 3 cm braking-lead cap. The existing predictor uses 40 ms,
180 ms and 2 cm respectively. The candidate remains an offline experiment;
it has not replaced the robot's braking function.

| Method on the held-out cases | Median absolute true error | Within 1 cm | Overshoots |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Existing predictor | 0.76 cm | 9/16 | 7 |
| Conservative candidate | 0.30 cm | 14/16 | 2 |

Two candidate cases still overshot by about 1.14–1.16 cm. Sixteen cases are a
small sample, and these results do not establish physical success rates.
[Paired candidate results](/home/jetbot/jetbot/local_nav/goals/braking-candidate-holdout-2026-09-14.json).

## Faster tracking: 95 recorded frame pairs

The benchmark excludes camera acquisition, socket communication, logging and
high-level planning. Two 250-feature baselines bracketed the lower-feature runs
to expose timing drift.

| Feature budget | Median tracker time | 95th-percentile time | Rejected pairs | Maximum translation change from baseline |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 250, first baseline | 55.4 ms | 62.1 ms | 0/95 | — |
| 125 | 46.6 ms | 51.5 ms | 0/95 | 0.0147 cm |
| 80 | 42.3 ms | 45.9 ms | 0/95 | 0.0254 cm |
| 250, repeated baseline | 49.3 ms | 54.9 ms | 0/95 | 0 cm |

125 features were approximately 6–16% faster than the two baselines. The minimum
accepted feature count was 121, and maximum yaw change was 0.0075°. This supports
trying 125 features next, but does not establish robustness on low-texture carpet
or scenes dominated by objects. The 250-feature default is preserved.

A separate thread-count comparison favored four OpenCV threads: median tracker
times were 81.8 ms with one thread, 65.6 ms with two, and 57.7 ms with four. I did
not reduce the thread count. Timing measurements were not run alongside the
CPU-heavy simulation campaigns.
[Feature-budget results](/home/jetbot/jetbot/local_nav/goals/feature-budget-benchmark-2026-09-14.json).

## Stress tests and a calibration blind spot

Fifteen scenarios ran at both 250 and 125 features, before and after the settling
fix: 60 runs total. The feature budgets produced the same outcome classifications.
The following values refer to the post-fix runs.

| Scenario | Observed behavior |
|---|---|
| Normal 10/15 cm routes | Estimated arrival; true final error about +0.12 cm in these particular cases |
| Slow 15 cm route | Stopped at the two-second powered limit, short of target |
| Low texture | Rejected before any motor command |
| Stalled motor | Stopped on the 400 ms no-progress guard |
| Sensor failure | Stopped; did not report arrival |
| Camera freeze | Independent motor lease expired; delayed controller then stopped |
| External stop | Rejected subsequent renewal with the stale control token |
| IMU drift | Stopped when the estimated pose left the allowed envelope |
| Coarse frame rate | Reported overshoot; true error about +1.60 cm |
| Slow processing | After the settling fix, correctly reported overshoot instead of an unverified final position; true error about +3.28 cm |
| Exposure steps/read noise | Tracking continued, but the chosen 5 cm scenario still overshot by about +1.12 cm |
| Camera height wrong by 15% | **Reported estimated arrival despite true error of about +1.12 cm** |

The last result matters: a stable image and good motion fit cannot verify absolute
metric scale. A wrong camera height biases monocular position while leaving a
plausible estimate. The controller's `goal_reached` is an estimated arrival under
its calibration assumptions, not independent proof of a physical position.
I have not hidden this failure or weakened the acceptance criteria.
[Stress results before the fix](/home/jetbot/jetbot/local_nav/goals/simulation-stress-2026-09-14.json)
and [after the fix](/home/jetbot/jetbot/local_nav/goals/simulation-stress-after-2026-09-14.json).

## Limits and next work

The synthetic scenes use a flat static carpet and mostly exact IMU attitude.
The stress suite adds yaw drift and wrong height, but there is still no model
of wheel slip, uneven carpet, contact, the charging cable, or unexpected objects.
The simulator shares the calibrated camera projection with the tracker. The
independent plant gives useful position ground truth, but this is not a substitute
for testing against an external physical distance reference.

When you are ready to resume supervised, untethered motion, the next useful
experiment is a paired 250-versus-125-feature run with external distance marks
and measured camera-to-command delay. Then test the conservative braking
candidate against actual coasting distance. Keep the existing margins and bounded
routes until those measurements support a change. Longer unattended routes and
continuous turns are not validated by this report.
